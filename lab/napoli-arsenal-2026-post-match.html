Napoli–Arsenal 0-1: the block keeps the scoreline close, not the game under control.

Napoli stay in the scoreline for a long time, but Arsenal control territory and final-third connections. The right side and Ødegaard become the centre of the game; the goal, after 88 seconds and 29 passes, is its clearest summary.

The scoreline stays close. The game much less so

The 0-1 suggests a match that remained balanced until the end. The pitch and the data show an equilibrium that existed much more in the scoreline than in the rest of the game.

Arsenal finish with 26 shots to 5. Opta Analyst’s expected-goals model puts the gap at 4.05 xG to 0.22. Napoli stay alive until the final whistle, but rarely manage to move the match onto their own terms.

This was exactly the question before kick-off. Allegri was likely to accept long spells without the ball and ask his side to defend in a medium-low block. The risk was that compactness could turn into passivity and allow Arsenal to move the block until their preferred connections appeared.

Napoli keep the scoreline level for a long time. They do not, however, remove the context in which Arsenal want to build the final pass.

Napoli choose the risk of waiting

In the first half Napoli’s 42 defensive actions describe a very deep team. The block height is measured at 15.6 metres from their own goal. Arsenal, when defending, occupy a much higher zone: 49.2 metres.

This is not a tactical surprise. Allegri does not build the match around constant high pressing. Napoli prefer to protect the centre, keep the opponent in front of them and choose the moments in which to step out.

The issue is therefore not the decision to defend deep in itself. The issue is what Arsenal are able to do while Napoli wait.

Depth is protected. The areas for the final pass much less so. As the minutes pass, Arsenal’s possession does not simply circulate around the block: it progressively finds the relationships needed to play through it.

Arsenal find their right side again

Before the match, the right side was the most stable territorial pattern in the Arsenal sample. At the Maradona it becomes even more pronounced.

Arsenal record 75 final-third entries. Of those, 48 come from the right: 64%. Fourteen come through the centre and 13 from the left. Eighteen entries also end in the right half-space.

This is not simply about getting the ball to Saka. White, Rice and Ødegaard keep offering passing lines around the winger, forcing Napoli to shift and repeatedly decide which space to protect.

In the pre-match sample, right-side entries ranged between 57% and 58%. Against Napoli that rises to 64%. Napoli knew where Arsenal were likely to build. Knowing where was not enough to stop it.

Ødegaard stays outside the box, not outside the game

It is probably the most important number in the entire analysis.

Ødegaard receives the ball 96 times. As many as 60 receptions come in the final third. Inside the box, however, he records none. He is found by 13 different team-mates.

At first glance, zero receptions in the box could look like a defensive success. In reality it tells only part of the story.

The pre-match issue was not to keep Ødegaard generically far from goal. It was to prevent him from receiving between the lines, facing forward and with immediate connections available. Napoli do not manage that consistently enough.

Napoli manage to keep him outside the box. They do not manage to keep him outside the game.

Arsenal’s possession reaches useful areas

Describing the match as simple Arsenal possession would be reductive. The ball circulates a lot, but it also keeps progressing towards the zones from which Arsenal can create.

Ødegaard attempts 108 passes, completes 93 at 86%, produces 11 progressive passes, 6 key passes and 13 passes to team-mates inside the box.

White and Rice complete the structure. White provides continuity on the right, while Rice repeatedly connects the base of possession with Ødegaard and the more advanced players.

The 4.05 xG helps put that possession in perspective. This is not territorial dominance without chances. It is possession that moves Napoli, reaches useful areas and accumulates shots.

The pass network makes the mechanism visible

In the first half, the most frequent connections look almost like a picture of the pre-match thesis.

White–Rice: 24. Rice–Ødegaard: 22. White–Ødegaard: 16. Saka–White: 15. Saka–Ødegaard: 15.

Not every move has to end on the right. It is enough for Napoli to keep shifting towards that zone, changing their distances and making decisions.

In the second half, some players and connections change but the principle does not. Rice–Ødegaard rises to 25 reciprocal passes, White–Ødegaard remains at 15 and the captain continues to be one of the central nodes even as the structure around him is reorganised.

88 seconds and 29 passes

The goal in the 75th minute is almost a summary of the entire match.

Arsenal establish possession at 73:10. Ødegaard scores at 74:38. Between those moments, 88 seconds pass and Arsenal complete 29 consecutive passes.

73:10 → 74:38 · 29 passes · 88 seconds. Arsenal keep the ball without interruption: the sequence starts with Gabriel, goes all the way back to Raya, progresses again through Rice and Bruno Guimarães and gradually moves to the right, where Ødegaard becomes increasingly involved.

The sequence does not race directly towards goal. Gabriel starts the possession, Hincapié returns the ball all the way to Raya and Arsenal build again from the back. From there, circulation advances through Konsa, Rice and Bruno Guimarães, alternating between the centre and the right side without giving Napoli a clear moment to break out of the block.

Ødegaard becomes a constant part of the move from 73:53 and completes 7 of the 29 passes before the finish. Bruno Guimarães completes six and Rice five. In the final phase Arsenal insist on the right again: White finds Ødegaard, Ødegaard plays to Tzolis and the ball quickly returns to the captain, who finishes the move.

The goal does not come from a sudden transition. It is the end point of a long possession against a set defence. That is why the length of the move matters: Arsenal can rebuild, shift the block several times and keep searching for the same relationships until the decisive space opens.

Ødegaard was already involved in almost everything

The goal does not suddenly create Ødegaard’s importance. It confirms it.

The captain is involved in 16 sequences that end with a shot: he takes three shots, directly creates six shots for team-mates and is involved in the previous pass on seven more occasions.

Saka remains heavily involved, but Ødegaard is the centre of gravity. The goal puts his name on the scoresheet. The data show that his influence was already spread across the entire match.

The plan keeps the game open, but never shifts it

Napoli do not lose because they choose to defend deep. That choice reduces depth, keeps the scoreline open and forces Arsenal to build patiently against a set defence.

The limitation appears within that same plan. The 64% of entries from the right, the 60 Ødegaard receptions in the final third and the pass network built around White, Rice, Saka and the captain all describe the same problem from different angles: Arsenal keep finding their strong side and their key player without having to change the nature of the match.

The goal sequence takes that dynamic to its extreme. Eighty-eight seconds and 29 passes, with the ball going backwards and starting again several times before the finish. Arsenal do not need to force the play. They can keep moving the block until a distance opens, a pressing action arrives late or Ødegaard finds another useful reception.

The lesson for Napoli is not that they should have pressed high for ninety minutes. It is more subtle: against a team able to manage possession with this continuity, defending deep works only if compactness is accompanied by the ability to break the rhythm, choose when to jump and make receptions between the lines uncomfortable.

The 0-1 remains narrow on the scoreboard. The gap is much less narrow in the way the two teams manage to control where and how the match is played.
