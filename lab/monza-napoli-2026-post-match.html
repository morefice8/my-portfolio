Monza–Napoli 1‑4: Napoli gave up the ball, not the match.

After the 2-1 Napoli left the ball to Monza for fourteen minutes: Monza had the pitch, but not the box, and shot twice from outside it. Then a corner, a throw-in and the ball back with Napoli.

Monza–Napoli ended 1-4 on Wednesday 28 October, at the Brianteo. Napoli, with Landucci on the bench because of Allegri’s suspension, won after the 3-3 with Roma and go up to 14 points; Monza stay on 7. It was a match of two injuries, Lobotka at 17′ and Rrahmani at the break, and of four Napoli goals in four different ways, the last by De Bruyne, who came on at 63′ and scored at 67′.

The pre-match said two things: that Monza, when they concede first, do not take points, and that Napoli, when they go ahead, give up the ball, with the quarter of an hour after the break as the meeting point. It went that way, but with an ending we had not written: Napoli gave up the ball for fourteen minutes, and Monza did nothing with it. Napoli gave up the ball, not the match.

The game, phase by phase

The simplest way to read it is to split it between one goal and the next, and look at possession. Here possession is each team’s share of touches: over the whole match Napoli are at 63%. They start on equal terms, go ahead at 10′ and for more than half an hour have 57%; Monza equalise at 43′; after the 2-1, at 48′, Napoli drop to 49%, and it is the only phase in which they have less of the ball than Monza. With the 3-1 the ball is theirs again: 61%, then 76%, and in the last minutes 81%.

Giving up the ball is not the same as being dominated. To understand the difference you have to take the match in order.

The goalkeeper, and nobody pressing

Napoli score at 10′ after fifteen seconds of build-up. Milinković-Savić (32), in goal in place of Meret, as Sky had said, plays it short; the ball goes from Rrahmani (13) to Di Lorenzo (22) and to Marín (16), then Di Lorenzo plays it long in behind, for 33 metres, and Højlund (19) scores from fifteen metres. Monza’s first intervention, a tackle by Colombo (21) on Anguissa (99), comes seven seconds after the goalkeeper’s pass and fails.

It was the pre-match prediction: Monza are not Gasperini’s Roma and do not go after the defenders. They allow 22.1 passes before each intervention, against 15.7 in the previous eight games; in their own box Napoli have 4% of touches followed by an intervention, 7 out of 167, against 11% over the whole match.

Monza, though, are not a team that just takes it. In the first half they shoot nine times, Napoli six, and at 43′ they equalise: twenty-two seconds of possession, from the right side to the left, then Robinson (46) crosses from the byline and Varela (9) scores with a header from five metres. It is Monza’s only big chance in the whole match. Birindelli (19) is also involved in the goal, but he is not the wing-back we had pointed to: he is the right-sided centre-back of the back three, and his pass is the second in the chain. Jurić spoke of “an excellent first half” and of Milinković-Savić who “saved the result 3 or 4 times”: the saves are four in all, three in the first half.

Two minutes and fifty-eight seconds

The second half begins with two substitutions: for Napoli, Marianucci (35), making his first league appearance of the season, for Rrahmani, who went off with a knock to the sternum according to DAZN’s commentary; for Monza, Carboni (44) for Goglichidze (2), booked at 8′. Napoli had already lost Lobotka (68) at 17′, after a contact with Mangas (7): Gilmour (6) comes on.

The 2-1 comes after two minutes and fifty-eight seconds. Milinković-Savić launches it long, 42 metres; Carboni, who has just come on, clears it badly; Di Lorenzo recovers it and plays it into the box, Politano (21) tries to dribble past and is stopped by Carboni, the ball stays there and Mangas makes a mistake at the edge of his own box: Politano scores from sixteen metres. Fourteen seconds from the long ball to the goal. Opta records Mangas’s mistake two seconds before the shot.

In the pre-match Monza were the team that scores right after the break: four of their fifteen goals had come between 46′ and 51′. This time it is the opposite. Jurić, to DAZN: “The first half was truly excellent and the start of the second half was even better. Then we gave away the second and the third goal.”

Monza have the pitch, not the box

After the 2-1 Napoli do what the pre-match said they do: they give up the ball. From 48′ to 62′ they have 49% of the touches, 70 against 74, defend 23 metres from their own goal against 36 in the long phase at 1-0, and touch the ball in the final third seven times, Monza twenty-three. Monza move up: their interventions are 61 metres from their own goal, in Napoli’s half.

But Monza have the pitch, not the box. In that quarter of an hour they have three touches in the box, like Napoli, and two shots: Foe Ondoa (8) from 22 metres at 54′, saved, and Colpani (28) from 32 metres at 61′, off target. No big chance. Napoli, with less of the ball, have two: Højlund at 61′, saved, and Marín’s goal.

That is the difference with Roma. Against Roma, in eleven minutes, Napoli had touched the ball 24 times against 118, with 37 Roma touches in the final third against one; here possession is almost level and Monza have twenty-three touches in the final third against seven. The ball alone is not enough: what counts is where you have it. Asked about Lang (70) “as a fifth midfielder”, Landucci answered: “When the others have the ball you have to defend.”

The 3-1 comes from a corner, at 62′. Politano takes it from the right, Marín wins the aerial duel with Varela and scores with a header from five metres, three seconds after the corner kick. It is Napoli’s third goal from a corner in the twelve games analysed, after Rrahmani against Villarreal and Gilmour against Fiorentina, and the sixth of the 22 goals conceded by Monza that comes from a corner. In the pre-match we had written that Marín was Napoli’s weak point on corners, with 36% of aerial duels won: he wins two out of five, but the decisive one.

The ball goes back to Napoli

After the 3-1 Napoli have the ball again: 61% of the touches until the 4-1, 76% until 81′, 81% at the end. At 63′ Landucci brings on De Bruyne (11) and Vergara (26) for Anguissa and Politano; at 67′ comes the 4-1, from a throw-in near the corner flag: McTominay (8) takes it, Lang crosses and De Bruyne scores from fourteen metres, four minutes after coming on. Allegri had said so on the eve of the match: “with De Bruyne it is more vertical, he switches the play in an instant” and “substitutions become fundamental”.

Monza, meanwhile, disappear: after Colpani’s shot at 61′ they do not shoot again. That is almost thirty-two minutes of play without a shot, while Napoli take eight. They have 24% of the touches between 67′ and 81′ and 19% at the end, and do not press: the PPDA is 22.1 over the whole match.

Allegri, on the eve of the Monza match, had said that against Roma Napoli had “forced it when they could have managed possession”. Here, when ahead, they have 64% of the touches against 57% when level: seven points more, whereas in the previous eight games it was eleven less. It should be read with caution: Monza, two and then three goals down, never pressed. And on 31 October, on the eve of Juventus, Allegri said of the team that it “must improve in managing the ball when we are ahead”. The quarter of an hour between the 2-1 and the 3-1 is, in the data, the moment that most resembles it.

The tactics board, with the real positions

In the pre-match we had drawn Napoli with Giovane (23) on the left and Monza with Carboni, Akinsanmiro and Mota. For Napoli it went almost entirely right: ten out of eleven, with Lang (70) in place of Giovane. For Monza eight out of eleven, but with a different role: Birindelli (19) is the right-sided centre-back and on the right there is Bakoune (24); Colombo (21) and Foe Ondoa play in the middle, up front Ngonge (16) and Varela behind Robinson.

The figure shows where the players touched the ball in the quarter of an hour after the 2-1, compared with the previous phase. Monza move up with the whole team: Birindelli touches the ball 39 metres higher than before, Bakoune 40, Colombo 26. Napoli drop deeper: Marín from 36 to 30 metres from his own goal, Marianucci (35) at 33; up front stay Højlund, Lang and Politano, the most advanced. These are few events per player, from three to thirteen: it is a snapshot of where the match is played, not a precise measurement.

Four goals, sixteen shots

Napoli score four goals from sixteen shots and four big chances; Monza have twelve shots and one big chance. Three of Napoli’s four goals do not come from a positional attack: a mistake after a long ball, a corner, a throw-in. Only the 1-0 comes from build-up from the back.

Monza shoot more in the first half, nine times against six, and less in the second, three times against ten. No shot after 61′. Jurić, at the end of the match, said: “We don’t have the perception of danger.” And to Sky: “Against these teams you pay for mistakes.”

Six players, what happened

In the pre-match we had pointed to six players to watch. Here they are, with two others who decided the game.

Varela (9) was the one to watch: he scores with a header, one goal from six shots, and now has 8 out of 22, 36% against 44% in the pre-match. He takes six of Monza’s twelve shots and wins three aerial duels out of seven. Jurić said that “during the week he works like a madman” and that “it’s truly unbelievable”.

Birindelli (19) was supposed to be the side to watch, and he is the right-sided centre-back: one key pass and the shot at 1′, saved.

Mangas (7) makes no key passes, and Opta records his mistake before the 2-1.

De Bruyne (11) starts on the bench, as Sky had said, and in thirty minutes scores and completes 43 passes out of 48.

Rrahmani (13) goes off at the break after a single aerial duel, won.

Politano (21) scores the 2-1 and takes the corner for the 3-1: goal and assist, with five crosses and three key passes in 62 minutes.

Marín (16) is the second decisive player: he scores with a header, wins two aerial duels out of five and touches the ball 112 times, more than anyone else at Napoli.

Milinković-Savić (32) makes four saves and plays 55 passes, 44 completed: twice, for the 1-0 and the 2-1, he is the first link in the goal.

What we said

Eleven pre-match questions, one by one. Four right: Monza without points when they concede first (now zero out of six), Monza not pressing, Varela (36% of shots, no longer 44%) and De Bruyne’s substitution. Three partly right: Napoli giving up the ball when ahead, but only between 48′ and 61′; the corners, which Monza still concede, even though there were only two corners; the tactics board, ten out of eleven for Napoli and eight out of eleven for Monza. Three wrong: the quarter of an hour after the break as Napoli’s worst moment, with two shots against two and the 2-1 after 2′58″; Monza scoring right after the break; Birindelli’s side. One the other way round: Marín.

After the whistle

Napoli lose Lobotka: the club’s diagnosis, on 29 October, is a tear of the tibiofibular syndesmosis without instability, associated with a low-grade tear of the anterior talofibular ligament of the left ankle; rehabilitation has already begun. Allegri, on 31 October, spoke of a return “in about 6 weeks” and said that after the international break Napoli will get back Spinazzola, Neres and Favasuli. Landucci, at the press conference, replied to the criticism: “Before they were on our backs about the ‘corto muso’, now we score four goals and it’s not right.”

On the Monza side, Jurić said that in defence “we are still at the ABC in certain situations” and that for him “it is very strange to concede all these goals”: it is 22 in nine games. Folorunsho, absent, has a calf problem. After the Brianteo the calendar took Monza to Bologna, on Saturday 31 October, and Napoli to Turin to face Juventus, on Sunday 1 November: this analysis stops at the final whistle of Monza–Napoli.

Gave up the ball, not the match

Napoli did what the pre-match said they do: they dropped deeper when ahead, for fourteen minutes. But Monza, who had the ball, did not find the box, and Napoli scored three times without a built-up attack: a Monza mistake after a long ball, a corner, a throw-in. From the 3-1 the ball was theirs again and Monza did not shoot any more.

The signal to take away: not how much of the ball you have when you are ahead, but where the team that has it has it.
