Serie A after 11 matchdays: the table doesn’t tell the whole story.

With the data of all 110 matches: Lazio have twelve and a half points more than their chances are worth, Napoli three and a half fewer. Five maps to understand what kind of teams they are, and Napoli’s current spell, which lies not in the chances but in the second half.

Eleven matchdays have gone by and the league stops for the international break. Napoli restart on Saturday 21 November against Torino, after eight days nobody would want to live again: the 0-2 against Juventus, the 1-4 at Porto, the 1-1 with Lazio, which the Maradona booed. Before preparing for the match we did something different: we took the events of all 110 matches played so far, of all twenty teams, and looked at them through the same lens.

There is one question: who plays better, or worse, than the table says? Points are what happened; the chances created and conceded are what the matches deserved to give. After eleven matchdays the two can be far apart, and the table doesn’t tell the whole story.

Real points, expected points

For every shot we calculated what it is worth, in xG: the probability that it becomes a goal, based on where it is taken from, with the head or the foot, and on what kind of move it comes from, a corner, a throw-in or a counter-attack. It is a model of our own, built on these matches: the values do not match those of other sites and should not be compared with theirs. With the xG of the two teams in every match we then calculated expected points: how many points, on average, the chances created and conceded are worth.

Lazio are top with 29 points. Their matches are worth 16.5: they have 12.5 more. They have scored 25 goals from chances worth 16.2 xG. Part of the gap can be real, because the model looks at where the shots are taken, not who takes them or who saves them. But chance alone moves a team by about four points over eleven matches, up or down: Lazio are more than three times that distance.

On the other side there are four teams six points or more below: Monza, with 7 points against the 15.2 of their chances, Fiorentina, Venezia and Udinese. Inter and Roma, second and third in the table, are first and second for expected points, 24.6 and 21.4: there the table and the play match.

And Napoli? They have 15 points, tenth place; their matches are worth 18.4, the fifth-highest total in Serie A. They are missing 3.4, and 3.4 is within normal variation. It is not an exceptional case: it is a team that has taken a little less than it created. Expected points say how it could have gone, not how it went: defeats remain defeats, and we will get to them.

Who they are: chances created and conceded

The five maps that follow divide the teams by the league median, the dashed lines. The thin bars are the statistical error after eleven matches: if two teams are closer than one bar, they should not be read as different. We left out the measures that are too noisy, such as the share of shots from set pieces or from counter-attacks.

The first map sets xG created against xG conceded. Inter are alone at the top right: 2.52 created and 0.97 conceded, per match. Roma are the second complete team. Juventus have the best defence in the league, 0.93, but a mid-table attack; Frosinone do the opposite, third-best attack and worst defence, 2.08 conceded, and are the team that plays the most open matches.

Napoli are at the top right, close to the line: fourth-best attack, 1.83, and ninth-best defence, 1.41. By difference they are fifth, in a group of five teams with Juventus, Como, Milan and Bologna, where the gaps are a few hundredths: behind Inter and Roma there is a group, not a hierarchy.

How they build: possession and verticality

Here possession is each team’s share of touches; verticality is the share of open-play passes that advance ten metres or more. Como, Inter and Milan have more of the ball than anyone, 60%, 59% and 58%, and move it forward calmly. Frosinone, Lecce and Parma are on the other side: they play direct, Frosinone with 32% of passes vertical and 47% of the touches.

Roma are the only team to combine a lot of the ball, 56%, and high verticality, 27%, and they are also the second most aggressive team without the ball: against them Napoli drew 3-3. Napoli sit in the middle of everything: 52% of the touches, seventh, and 25.9% of passes vertical, practically the median. It is not a team you can recognise from these two numbers, and perhaps that is already news.

How they press: intensity and height

PPDA counts how many passes a team allows per defensive action: the lower it is, the more it presses. Frosinone are the most aggressive, 9.1, ahead of Roma and Juventus. Sassuolo, Cagliari and Atalanta are the most passive, and Torino are the fourth most passive. Roma, Inter and Juventus are also the teams that defend highest, over 40 metres from their own goal; Parma and Cagliari the lowest.

Napoli press exactly like the league median, 14.8, and defend a little lower, at 32 metres against the median’s 34. It is the most uncertain map, because block height has the longest bars: it is useful for spotting the extremes, not for ranking the teams in the middle.

The attack: volume and quality

Volume divides the teams: Inter, Frosinone and Roma shoot more than all the others, between 19 and 21 shots per match. Quality divides less, and in a different way: Bologna and Milan have the best shots, 0.12 xG each, and Milan do it with only 11.5 shots per match, one of the lowest volumes in the league: few shots, but good ones.

Napoli are seventh for shots, 15.6, and seventh for quality, 0.12: above the median on both axes, which is a good picture of the attack of a top side. In the league they have scored 18 goals from 20.1 xG: two fewer than they created. The attack is not what is missing.

The defence: shots conceded and danger

At the other extreme, Inter, Juventus and Milan concede fewer than 12 shots per match; Parma, Cagliari, Atalanta and Frosinone more than 18. Napoli concede 16.2: the sixth-highest figure in the league, level with Venezia.

But these are shots of a different kind. The xG per shot conceded is 0.08, the lowest value in Serie A; Napoli’s opponents shoot from 19.4 metres on average, the longest distance; only 52% of the shots against come from inside the box, against 63% for the median, and big chances are 0.8 per match, against 2.7: only Juventus do better. It is a profile consistent with a defence at 32 metres: it lets teams shoot, but from far out. In the league the goals conceded are 14, against 15.5 xG conceded. Napoli’s defence, in the league, is not the problem.

Napoli, fifteen matches

So where are the points? To answer, we have to put the last eight days in order. On 1 November, at Juventus’s ground, Napoli lose 0-2: Højlund (19) is out with the flu and Lucca (20) plays as centre-forward, De Bruyne (11) and Di Lorenzo (22) do not come back out after the break, Juventus score at 70′ with a shot from outside the box and at 87′ with Boga (13). According to our data it is the match with the fewest xG for Napoli in the league, 0.63.

On 4 November in Porto it ends 1-4, the third defeat in four Champions League matches. At 10′ Bednarek (5) puts Porto ahead with a header from a corner, at 42′ McTominay (8) equalises; in the second half Porto score on the counter-attack, again from a corner and from a penalty, and Rafa Marín (16) is sent off. After the match McTominay said, to Prime Video: “We concede too many goals, that’s the story. There are too many injuries at the moment, they happen in football but we really have too many.” Højlund, as reported by the Gazzetta dello Sport: “We have to improve, we are really in a dark moment of our season.”

On 6 November, according to the reports, De Laurentiis arrived at Castel Volturno, where the team was in training camp ahead of Lazio. On 8 November Napoli draw 1-1 at the Maradona: Frattesi (16) scores at 28′, an own goal by Pedraza (23) from a cross by Lang (70) equalises at 48′, and the crowd boos. Allegri, to DAZN, said that “we are behind in the table but not all is lost” and that the team having pulled together “was a small brick we have put in place.”

What the numbers see

In fifteen matches, across the league and the Champions League, Napoli have 5 wins, 3 draws and 7 defeats. Of the seven, they lost three while creating more than the opponent: against Como, against Venezia and at Villarreal. The other four, Inter, Arsenal, Juventus and Porto, are defeats in which the opponent created more.

And there is a difference between the two competitions. In Serie A Napoli create 1.83 xG per match and concede 1.41. In the Champions League, in the four matches, they create 1.4 and concede 2.3, with 11 goals conceded: the two numbers flip. The difficult spell, in this data, is also European.

The second half

Politano (21), on Sky after Porto, pointed to the same issue that the data point to: “We ask ourselves too why we concede so many goals in the last half hour. Sometimes, more than a tactical matter, we lack that ruthlessness you need so as not to concede goals. We hardly feel the danger.” The numbers prove him right: of the 25 goals conceded in fifteen matches, 17 came in the second half and 14 after 60′, in seven matches out of fifteen.

In the first half Napoli are better than their opponent, 13.5 xG created against 8.5 conceded; in the second they are worse, 12.0 against 16.3. The lowest point is the quarter of an hour after the break: 32 shots against 49, while in the first fifteen minutes of the match the xG were 5.1 against 2.2.

But it is not a constant collapse. In eleven matches out of fifteen Napoli concede, in the second half, half an xG on average. In four they concede more than two: against Inter, Arsenal, Bodø and Porto. That is 10.3 of the 16.3 xG conceded in the second halves. Nor are these matches in which Napoli were chasing: at the break they were not losing in any of them, twice 0-0, once 1-1 and once ahead 2-1. When the second half breaks, it breaks hard.

There is a clue to follow, not a rule. Twice, when ahead, Napoli switched to five defenders during the match: against Inter at 79′, at 2-1, and against Bodø at 78′. Inter equalised at 81′ and won at 90′; Bodø scored at 93′. That is two matches: enough to say what to look at, not what to think. In general Napoli have changed often: four different starting systems in fifteen matches, a back three in five, and a change of system during the match in six.

The match against Lazio, by contrast, went as Allegri and Rrahmani described it, both to DAZN. Rrahmani: “in the first half we got into a bit of difficulty, while in the second half we were good and could even have won.” The numbers agree: in the first half 5 shots against 12, in the second 10 against 3 and 0.8 xG against 0.3.

What the numbers don’t say

The newspapers, after Porto, reported a divided dressing room, an argument on the pitch between Lucca and De Bruyne and Politano’s words about the explanations given at the end of the match. These are press reconstructions, with sources not always named. Event data measure where shots are taken and where a team defends, not a group: we cannot say whether the dressing room is united or not, and we do not say it.

What can be said lies in the facts and in the numbers. In the league Napoli create more than they concede and take about three points fewer than their chances are worth, a normal distance. In the Champions League they concede too much. In four matches out of fifteen the second half broke down, and the players themselves, from McTominay to Politano, point to it as a problem of goals conceded. Every defeat has its own story: adding a single story to all seven would be a mistake.

Torino, the next opponent

Napoli restart from Torino, on Saturday 21 November. Torino have 14 points, one fewer than Napoli. They create little: 10.6 shots per match, the nineteenth-ranked figure in the league, and 1.03 xG, the eighteenth. They press little: 16.9 passes allowed per defensive action, the fourth most passive team. They defend at 32 metres, like Napoli, and their goalkeeper plays long almost every other time, the sixth in the league. On possession and on verticality they are in the median.

For Napoli this is an opponent that does not press and gives them time. In the pre-match we will look at what happens when Napoli have that time, and what happens after the break.

How these numbers are made

The data are the match events of all 110 Serie A matches played after eleven matchdays, plus Napoli’s four Champions League matches. The xG model is a logistic regression on shot position, angle, header and the origin of the move. Evaluated out of sample, match by match, it separates the shots that become goals with an area under the curve of 0.74, where 0.5 would be chance. Penalties are valued at the league rate, own goals have no xG.

Expected points put two independent distributions side by side, one per team, match by match: this is a simplification, because the goals in a match are not truly independent. Measures with too much noise after eleven matches, such as the share of shots from set pieces and counter-attacks, are not on the maps; block height and xG per shot conceded are uncertain, and the bars say so. The numbers will be recalculated after every matchday.

The table is a snapshot

Napoli do not play like a tenth-place team: they create like a top team, concede shots from far out, have an attack above the median and take about three points fewer than their chances are worth, a gap within chance. What is not chance lies elsewhere: in the Champions League, and in four second halves in which the match broke apart.

The signal to take away: not how many points Napoli have, but what happens after the break.
