Villarreal–Napoli 3-1: three lost balls, three goals.

In the pre-match we pointed to the lost ball as Villarreal’s weak spot. It was Napoli who paid for it: all three Spanish goals come from a mistake of theirs in their own half, in a game in which they took almost every shot from inside the box.

Villarreal–Napoli ended 3-1. In the pre-match we wrote that the weak spot of the game was the lost ball and that Villarreal would pay for it the most.

The opposite happened. All three Villarreal goals came from a ball Napoli lost in their own half, 3, 10 and 25 seconds after the mistake.

But the game was not one-sided, and the most honest way to tell it is to follow it from the start. In the first 22 minutes Napoli went ahead and created the best chances.

Napoli start better

At 7′ Napoli were already ahead, from a short corner: De Bruyne to Di Lorenzo, back to De Bruyne, then Gilmour. His cross reached Rrahmani, who headed it: the goalkeeper deflected it onto the crossbar and Rrahmani scored the rebound.

At 21′ came the second hit of the woodwork, by Højlund, who struck the junction of post and bar from a few metres. In 22 minutes Napoli had shot four times, always from inside the box.

On the flanks too the game seemed to be turning their way. From the stadium the feeling was clear: at the start the Di Lorenzo–Giovane link caused Villarreal a lot of trouble and Napoli’s wing-backs were often free. The data point the same way: in the first 25 minutes Napoli completed 17 passes into the final third down the left, against 9 down the right, and Di Lorenzo and Giovane exchanged 20 passes in an hour.

Giovane was part of that link, and according to the pre-match press he was not even on the UEFA list. That was true until Monday afternoon: that day Napoli changed the list and put Giovane in place of Costantino Favasuli, injured with Italy Under-21 on 1 October (a second-degree tear of the rectus femoris, out until November, as Corriere dello Sport wrote). UEFA rules allow a player with a long-term injury to be replaced until matchday six of the league phase. When Favasuli has recovered he will return to the list and Giovane will come off.

Napoli played with a back three, Politano and Di Lorenzo on the flanks, Gilmour and McTominay in midfield (not Lobotka, as expected) and two number tens, De Bruyne and Giovane, behind Højlund. Villarreal, without Pépé, in a 4-4-2.

Three minutes that change everything

Then, in three minutes, the game turned upside down. The first goal came at 22′. Napoli had just won the ball and Politano tried a backward pass towards his own box. Mikautadze intercepted it and shot straight away: goal, three seconds after the mistake.

The second, at 25′, came while Napoli were trying to build out from the back. Lots of short passes between Rafa Marín, Rrahmani and McTominay, then a pass from Marín from the wing towards the centre that ended up with Villarreal. Ten seconds later Mouriño shot from distance, almost 30 metres, and scored.

There is a detail that makes it all more bitter: both goals come between the 20th and the 45th minute, the only one of the three phases of the game in which Napoli had more of the ball (58% of the passes). When you control the ball, every wrong pass out from the back becomes a gift. And Napoli build precisely from the defence: the most involved player was Rrahmani, with 109 passes played or received.

Villarreal, at that moment, were not pressing: in the first half they allowed many passes before stepping in. That is why the two mistakes look more like a passing choice than the result of constant pressure. In general Napoli built out well: they lost 10 build-ups out of 22, against Villarreal’s 17 out of 22.

Villarreal close ranks and wait

In the pre-match we asked which Villarreal would show up, the high one of the home games or the cautious one of Madrid. After 2-1 the answer was clear: they dropped back and waited. They pressed little, especially in the first half, and in the second half they dropped even deeper. Napoli defended higher and pressed more.

Pérez found the countermeasures without changing the players. From the stands it looked as if they slid across better and left Napoli’s wing-backs in one-on-ones. Here the data say something: on Politano’s flank, Carlos Romero made 8 tackles, more than anyone else (nobody else made more than three), five of them after the break. Politano tried 7 dribbles and completed 2, both in the second half: all five of the failed ones ended in a Romero tackle.

The engine of this waiting Villarreal was number 18, Gueye: 8 ball recoveries, more than anyone else in his team, and 93 passes played or received, second only to Veiga. He played 83 minutes and was replaced by Cardona.

Napoli, meanwhile, kept creating but not scoring. They shot almost always from close: 10 shots out of 11 from inside the box, against 7 of 16 for Villarreal. Sofascore’s xG says the same: Napoli 1.70, Villarreal 1.24. Villarreal scored with three of their four shots on target.

Napoli created more, Villarreal scored more. Mouriño’s shot from almost 30 metres also weighs on the mistake that came before it.

After De Bruyne’s shot at 43′, Napoli did not shoot again until 76′: more than half an hour without shooting, while Villarreal tried four times. Not everything can be seen with the data we have: to measure distances between lines, slides across and space left free, tracking data would be needed.

The last flurry, then the 85th minute

Allegri changed almost half the team: Lang, Spinazzola and Neres after the hour, then Lobotka and Lucca at 71′, with the defence moving to four. Villarreal answered with Oluwaseyi and Freeman at 62′ and Foyth at 67′.

Napoli had one last flurry: four shots in six minutes, between 76′ and 81′, all from inside the box. None went in.

The third goal came four minutes later. Badiashile won the ball near his own box and passed it to Milinković-Savić, who went long: ball lost. From there Villarreal needed 25 seconds. Mouriño played long down the wing, Freeman won the ball, then came Foyth, Oluwaseyi and Saliba, and Oluwaseyi again for the goal. Three of the players involved had come on from the bench.

In the pre-match we wrote that the game could be decided in the last quarter of an hour, and that whoever had more energy and better substitutions would win. The data don’t say whether Napoli were tired or struggling to concentrate: they only say that, for the third time in the same game, a ball lost near their own box became a goal.

Not the first time

Three goals from lost balls in the same game are a lot, but the mechanism is not new. In eight games Napoli have conceded ten goals and six came from an opposition ball recovery: three before Villarreal and three at Villarreal.

Against Como and Fiorentina the goal came after a misplaced Napoli pass, like Mikautadze’s first. Against Inter, instead, the problem was different: the three goals came from throw-ins and established attacks, not from lost balls.

In the pre-match we wrote that Villarreal had conceded nine goals out of twelve from a ball recovery, Napoli three out of seven. Now the numbers are six out of ten for Napoli and nine out of thirteen for Villarreal, who this time conceded just one goal, from a corner. Against Frosinone losing the ball in build-up had cost nothing. At Villarreal it cost three goals.

The right predictions and the wrong ones

Some we read well. Villarreal showed up in the cautious version, the weak spot in their build-up was really there, Veiga was again the most involved in his team and the ending decided the game. McTominay played as expected: starter, but for an hour.

Others not. Giovane started, Lobotka didn’t, and Villarreal attacked more from the left than from the right, with very few crosses. Above all, we read the risk from the wrong side: Villarreal pay for lost balls, the game confirms it, but this time it was Napoli who lost them in the wrong place.

Zero points after two matchdays

After Arsenal, Napoli are still on zero points after two Champions League matchdays. On territory and chances they were no worse than Villarreal: more crosses, shots from close, two hits of the woodwork. But creating is not enough if you then give the ball away near your own box.

On Saturday Napoli go to Venezia in the league (read the pre-match). What to watch will be the same: how they build out when ahead and after a mistake.

Napoli created more, but lost the ball three times in their own half and Villarreal made them pay three times.
